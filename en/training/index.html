How I teach

My experience as a researcher, university teacher and corporate trainer has taught me one thing: the quality of a training course is not measured by the number of slides covered, but by the participants' ability to apply what they learned to their own code.

I build my courses around hands-on exercises in Jupyter notebooks, with use cases close to the real problems participants face. I make sure each concept is understood before moving on to the next, even if it means adapting the pace to the group. I remain available for occasional questions in the weeks following the training: learning does not stop on the last day.

Agentic AI

Why train your teams in the age of agentic AI?

Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI, Aider: coding agents are transforming the engineering profession. A developer can now produce hundreds of lines of NumPy or Polars without mastering the fundamentals. This is precisely what makes training strategic today.

Models are structurally behind the libraries

Training a frontier model takes months, and data science libraries evolve constantly — new Polars APIs, changes in the streaming engine, changes in scikit-learn's behavior, new NumPy releases. Agents often generate code that looks plausible but relies on obsolete APIs, deprecated patterns, or ignores recent optimizations. Without a team able to spot these gaps, the code drifts silently.

Agentic AI amplifies gaps as much as skills

For an engineer who masters the subject, it is a real productivity booster: they know how to steer the agent, check its output and correct its drift. For a team that does not master the fundamentals, it is the opposite: a sink of unmaintainable code, where subtle, hard-to-fix bugs pile up. A recent CodeRabbit study, reported by Stack Overflow, shows that AI-generated code contains 1.7 times more bugs than human-written code, including 75% more logic and correctness issues — exactly the kind of errors that slip through code review and end up as production incidents.

My courses include the use of AI agents

I use these tools myself every day in my research work — Claude Code, GitHub Copilot and others — and I have a cross-cutting view of them. Beyond the Python and data science fundamentals, I teach how to get the most out of coding assistants in a professional setting: structuring requests to get quality code, reviewing and auditing the output efficiently, spotting suspicious patterns, and combining the agent with good testing and profiling practices. The goal: make AI a real productivity lever, while guaranteeing the maintainability and reliability of the code you ship.

Training your teams today gives them the critical skills that turn an AI agent into a real productivity lever, and a lasting competitive edge over teams that endure AI instead of steering it.

Frequently asked questions

Format and organization

Is the remote format suitable for this kind of training?

Yes, and it is a fair question for such technical courses. Remote training works well here for several reasons: the hands-on exercises are done on CoCalc, a collaborative cloud environment that lets me see each participant's code in real time and step in directly when someone is stuck — exactly as in person. Sessions are limited to 10 participants to ensure individual follow-up. The pace is adapted, with frequent time for questions. In our post-training surveys, 90% of participants rate the remote format as equivalent or better than in-person.

How long are the courses and how are they organized?

Each course lasts 3 days (21 hours), in continuous sessions. The in-company format lets you adapt the daily schedule to your constraints (3 consecutive or spaced-out days). The group is limited to 10 participants.

Can the training take place on our premises?

Yes. The courses are offered on site or remotely. On site, each participant needs a computer with Internet access and a large screen that comfortably fits two windows side by side, or a dual screen. The technical environment (CoCalc) requires no installation.

Content and level

Do participants need to know Python already?

It depends on the course. The Python data science course (NumPy, Pandas, Seaborn) assumes some prior Python experience but no data science. The high-performance data science course (Polars, Numba, parallelism) is for teams already comfortable with the data science ecosystem. If your team needs a Python refresher before starting, I also offer a dedicated Python level 1 course. A preliminary discussion lets us check that the program matches the team's level.

Can the courses be adapted to our team's context?

Yes. The in-company format makes it possible to adjust the content to your use cases, your data and your technical constraints. A scoping discussion before the training identifies the priorities and steers the exercises accordingly.

What is the difference between the three courses in the catalog?

The three courses cover distinct levels and goals: Python data science lays the foundations (NumPy, Pandas, visualization); high-performance data science in Python moves on to performance at scale (Polars, Numba, parallelism, memory management); the third course focuses on machine learning with scikit-learn, from prototype to production. They can be taken independently, depending on the team's level.

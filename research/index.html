Short Bio

I am a senior research scientist (directeur de recherche) at Inria Sophia Antipolis, France, in the project-team DIANA. I made two academic breaks: from 2000 to 2004, to join the startup Castify Networks as an architect and then CTO assistant, and from 2016 to 2020, to found the startup ElectroSmart.

I received my Ph.D. (docteur ès sciences) in Communication Systems in 2000 and my HDR (Habilitation à Diriger des Recherches) in 2012. I did my thesis work at Institut Eurecom, France, in Ernst Biersack's group, on multicast congestion control. I also received a master's degree in mathematics (maîtrise de mathématiques) from the Université de Nice-Sophia Antipolis, France.

In parallel, I provide in-company training in Python data science and data science consulting. My full career is on my home page.

Projects

Current

  • FoolPilot: how do wrong AI-style suggestions affect human decisions? They lower accuracy and inflate confidence in errors (preprint hal-05750531).
  • LaunderCoin: identifying Bitcoin addresses linked to illicit activity, starting with the addresses extracted from underground forum discussions (preprint arXiv:2608.13930).

Past

  • Greasy: Which traces do you leave on the Web?
  • ElectroSmart: Revealing your exposure to electromagnetic waves.
  • soTweet: Information propagation in social networks.
  • Meddle: Putting control of mobile devices and carriers in end users' hands.
  • Video Streaming: What are the traffic characteristics of video streaming?
  • Bluebear: Exploring privacy threats in the Internet.
  • P2P-CD: Understanding and improving P2P content delivery.
  • PLM: Efficient multicast congestion control.
